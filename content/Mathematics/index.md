> [!INFO]
> the study of **structure**, **quantity**, **space**, and **change** &rarr; a foundational language for **science**, **logic**, and **abstract reasoning**.

- Encompasses both **abstract theory** and applied **problem-solving**
- Bridges disciplines from [[Physics/index|Physics]] and [[Computer Science Introduction/index|Computer Science]] to **Economics** and **Engineering**
- Evolves through formal systems, proofs, and symbolic reasoning

## Purpose

- Develop frameworks for **understanding patterns, relationships, and transformations**
- Support modeling, prediction, and optimization across domains
- Enable rigorous reasoning and precise communication of ideas

## Benefits

- Enhances logical thinking and analytical precision
- Powers innovation in technology, science, and finance
- Enables abstraction and generalization across diverse problems

## Applications

- **[[Physics/index|Physics]]**  
  - Models motion, energy, and quantum systems  
- **[[Computer Science Introduction/index|Computer Science]]**  
  - Underpins algorithms, cryptography, and machine learning  
- **Economics**  
  - Supports modeling of markets, risk, and optimization  
- **Engineering**
  - Drives design, simulation, and control systems  

## Challenges

- Requires abstraction and symbolic fluency
- Demands precision in definitions, logic, and proof
- Can be conceptually dense without strong scaffolding

# Mathematical Paradigm

## [[Mathematics/Algebra/index|Algebra]]

- Rooted in **symbolic manipulation** and **equation solving**, dating back to Babylonian mathematics  
- Formalized through **abstract structures** like groups, rings, and fields  
- Introduced **variables**, **functions**, and **polynomial systems** as foundational tools  
- Evolved into **abstract algebra**, enabling generalization across mathematical systems  
- Powers everything from **cryptography** to **error-correcting codes** and **algebraic geometry**

## [[Mathematics/Statistics/index|Statistics]]

- Originated from **data collection** and **population studies** in the 18th century  
- Built on foundations of **probability theory**, **sampling**, and **distribution modeling**  
- Developed tools for **descriptive analysis**, **inferential reasoning**, and **hypothesis testing**  
- Expanded into **correlation**, **regression**, and **Bayesian inference**  
- Drives decision-making in **science**, **economics**, **medicine**, and **AI**

## [[Mathematics/Calculus/index|Calculus]]

- Formalized by **Newton** and **Leibniz** to study **rates of change** and **accumulated quantities**  
- Divided into **differential** and **integral** branches, unified by the **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus**  
- Extended to **multivariable calculus**, **vector fields**, and **differential equations**  
- Essential for modeling **motion**, **growth**, and **optimization** in physics and engineering  
- Underpins modern analysis, including **limits**, **continuity**, and **convergence**

## [[Mathematics/Trigonometry/index|Trigonometry]]

- Originated from ancient studies of **astronomy** and **geometry**  
- Explores relationships between **angles**, **side lengths**, and **circular motion**  
- Introduces **sine**, **cosine**, **tangent**, and their identities  
- Forms the basis for **wave theory**, **signal processing**, and **Fourier analysis**  
- Bridges geometry and algebra through **unit circle** and **complex numbers**
