> [!INFO]
> **Accountability and governance** are essential for ensuring that AI systems are developed and deployed **responsibly and transparently**.  
> Without clear oversight, it becomes difficult to determine **who is liable** when AI systems cause harm or produce unintended outcomes.

## Key Challenges

- **Ambiguous Responsibility**: Unclear liability across developers, manufacturers, and AI systems  
- **Lack of Oversight**: Absence of formal governance structures in many organizations  
- **Regulatory Gaps**: Rapid AI advancement outpaces existing legal frameworks  
- **Principle-to-Policy Gap**: Ethics guidelines often lack actionable enforcement mechanisms

**Example**: If an autonomous vehicle causes an accident, should liability fall on the manufacturer, software developer, or the AI model itself?

## Governance Structures

- **AI Ethics Boards**: Cross-disciplinary panels to evaluate ethical risks and guide development  
- **Regulatory Compliance Teams**: Ensure alignment with laws like the EU AI Act  
- **Internal Auditing Protocols**: Monitor model behavior and flag ethical violations  
- **Risk Classification**: Systems categorized by risk level to determine oversight intensity

## Public and Private Sector Roles

- **Governments**: Enacting legislation such as the **European Union’s AI Act**, which imposes strict requirements on high-risk applications  
- **Corporations**: Companies like **Google**, **Microsoft**, and **IBM** publish AI ethics guidelines focused on fairness, reliability, and user trust  
- **Policy Translation**: Moving from abstract principles to concrete policies, audits, and reporting mechanisms

## Lifecycle Accountability

- **Ethical Audits**: Regular evaluations of model behavior and societal impact  
- **Algorithmic Impact Assessments (AIA)**: Frameworks to assess fairness before deployment  
- **Reporting Channels**: Mechanisms for users and stakeholders to flag AI-related harms

## Case Study: Algorithmic Accountability in Hiring – Amazon’s AI Recruiting Tool

In **2018**, Amazon discontinued an AI-driven hiring tool after discovering it penalized resumes containing female-coded language, such as "women’s chess club."

**Responses**:  
- **Bias Discovery**: Revealed systemic bias from historical training data  
- **Tool Retirement**: Amazon scrapped the system to prevent discriminatory outcomes  
- **Industry Shift**: Organizations began adopting **Algorithmic Impact Assessments (AIA)** and bias auditing tools  
- **Ethics Teams**: Companies hired dedicated staff to oversee fairness in recruitment analytics
