# Ethical AI and Policy

## [[Artificial Intelligence/Ethical AI and Policy/Bias and Fairness|Bias and Fairness]]

> [!INFO]
> Addresses **systemic and algorithmic bias** in AI systems to promote **equitable outcomes** across diverse populations.

### Process

- Identify **bias sources** in data, model design, and feedback loops  
- Apply **fairness metrics** to evaluate model behavior  
- Implement **mitigation strategies** (e.g., reweighting, adversarial debiasing)  
- Document decisions and maintain **audit trails** for transparency

### Advantages

- Promotes **equitable treatment** across demographic groups  
- Enhances **trust** and **accountability** in AI systems  
- Supports **regulatory compliance** and ethical standards

### Disadvantages

- Trade-offs between **fairness and accuracy**  
- Complex to define fairness across **contexts and cultures**  
- Requires **ongoing monitoring** and **stakeholder input**

## [[Artificial Intelligence/Ethical AI and Policy/Data Privacy and Security|Data Privacy and Security]]

> [!INFO]
> Ensures that AI systems **respect user data rights** and maintain **robust protections** against misuse or breaches.

### Process

- Apply **data governance principles** (e.g., consent, minimization, anonymization)  
- Implement **encryption** and secure model deployment  
- Monitor for **adversarial threats** and **data leakage**  
- Align with legal frameworks (e.g., **GDPR**, **CCPA**)

### Advantages

- Protects **sensitive user information**  
- Reduces risk of **reputational** and **legal harm**  
- Builds **user trust** and system resilience

### Disadvantages

- May limit **data availability** for training  
- Adds complexity to **deployment workflows**  
- Requires **continuous updates** to meet evolving standards

## [[Artificial Intelligence/Ethical AI and Policy/Accountability and Governance|Accountability and Governance]]

> [!INFO]
> Provides **institutional structures and processes** for overseeing AI development, deployment, and accountability.

### Process

- Establish **ethics boards** and **review committees**  
- Define **escalation paths** and **decision protocols**  
- Create **transparency reports** and **stakeholder maps**  
- Align internal practices with **external regulations**

### Advantages

- Clarifies **roles and responsibilities**  
- Supports **proactive risk management**  
- Enables **cross-functional collaboration**

### Disadvantages

- Can slow down **innovation cycles**  
- Requires **organizational buy-in** and resources  
- May face resistance in **decentralized environments**

## [[Artificial Intelligence/Ethical AI and Policy/Informed Consent and User Autonomy|Informed Consent and User Autonomy]]

> [!INFO]
> Ensures individuals understand how their data will be used before sharing it, promoting **transparency**, **control**, and **user agency** in AI systems.

### Process

- Design **user-friendly privacy settings** and opt-in/opt-out controls  
- Implement **dynamic consent frameworks** that evolve with user preferences  
- Embed **privacy-by-design** principles into system architecture  
- Align with rights-based policies (e.g., **Right to Be Forgotten**, **HIPAA**, **GDPR**)

### Advantages

- Respects **individual autonomy** and **data ownership**  
- Enhances **transparency** and **user trust**  
- Supports **ethical alignment** and **legal compliance**

### Disadvantages

- Traditional consent models may be **ineffective** for passive data collection  
- Requires **ongoing user engagement** and interface design  
- Legal and ethical standards vary across **jurisdictions and technologies**

## [[Artificial Intelligence/Ethical AI and Policy/Policy Development/index|Policy Development]]

> [!INFO]
> Translates **ethical principles**, stakeholder values, and legal mandates into **operational governance policies** that guide responsible AI development and deployment.

### Process

- Map **regulatory requirements** (e.g., EU AI Act, Algorithmic Accountability Act) to internal workflows  
- Draft **organizational guidelines** for fairness, transparency, and accountability  
- Conduct **Algorithmic Impact Assessments (AIA)** to evaluate societal risks before deployment  
- Define **reporting mechanisms** for AI-related harms and ethical violations  
- Align governance policies with **technical implementation** across teams

### Advantages

- Bridges the gap between **abstract principles** and **practical enforcement**  
- Enhances **regulatory compliance** and internal consistency  
- Enables **scalable governance** across departments and systems  
- Supports **stakeholder trust** through documented accountability

### Disadvantages

- Requires **coordination** between legal, technical, and operational teams  
- May need frequent updates to reflect **evolving regulations and risks**  
- Implementation can be **resource-intensive**, especially for smaller organizations  
- Risk of **policy fragmentation** if not standardized across platforms
