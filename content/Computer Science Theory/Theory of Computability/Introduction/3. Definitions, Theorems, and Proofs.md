> [!abstract] Abstract
> Theoretical computer science relies on formal mathematical rigor to describe, verify, and reason about computation. Definitions, theorems, and proofs form the core framework for establishing absolute logical certainty.

---
## Structural Components

- **Definition:** A precise, unambiguous description of objects and notions, clearly establishing what constitutes an object.
- **Theorem:** A precise mathematical statement that has been proven true, expressing specific properties of mathematical objects.
- **Lemma:** A supporting mathematical statement whose primary value lies in assisting the proof of a major theorem.
- **Corollary:** A direct, easily derived statement that follows naturally from a theorem or its proof.

## Proof Framework

### The Nature of Mathematical Proofs

A **proof** is a convincing logical argument demonstrating that a mathematical statement is unconditionally true.

- **Standard of Rigor:** Unlike legal standards ("beyond a reasonable doubt") or natural science ("weight of evidence"), mathematical proof requires absolute logical certainty.
- **Role of Evidence:** Empirical evidence plays no role in mathematics; an argument is only valid if every step is logically indisputable.

### Finding and Constructing Proofs

Proof construction is a creative process of discovery rather than a mechanical, linear task.

1. **Understand the Statement:** Ensure full clarity on the exact definitions of all terms involved.
2. **Experiment:** Test small examples to build intuition for why the statement holds (or to locate potential counterexamples).
3. **Decompose:** Divide the core argument into smaller, manageable logical lemmas or sub-cases.
4. **Iterate with Patience:** Proof generation involves trial and error; explore alternative perspectives if an initial approach stalls.
