> [!abstract] Abstract
> Theoretical computer science is structured around three core pillars: Automata Theory, Computability Theory, and Complexity Theory. Together, they establish the formal models of computation and explore what computers can and cannot solve efficiently.

> [!question] Central Question
> **What are the fundamental capabilities and limitations of computers?**

---
# Theoretical Pillars

## Complexity Theory
Classifies problems by their computational difficulty (*easy vs. hard*). When confronting a problem that appears computationally difficult, several strategies exist:

- **Target Root Causes:** Identify the specific aspect causing difficulty and adjust constraints so the problem becomes tractable.
- **Approximation:** Settle for an approximate solution rather than an exact one when exact calculation is hard.
- **Average-Case Optimization:** Accept slow worst-case behavior if the algorithm runs quickly on typical inputs.
- **Alternative Paradigms:** Utilize non-traditional models, such as randomized computation, to speed up execution.

## Computability Theory
Classifies problems by feasibility (*solvable vs. unsolvable*).

- **Core Finding:** Certain fundamental problems (such as determining whether an arbitrary mathematical statement is true or false) cannot be solved by any computer.
- **Historical Impact:** The abstract models created to define computability directly laid the groundwork for actual physical computer architectures.
- **Distinction from Complexity:** 
	- [[#Computability Theory|Computability]] classifies problems by how *difficult* it is.
	- [[#Complexity Theory|Complexity]] classifies solvable problems by *how hard* they are to solve.

## Automata Theory
Deals with the formal definitions and theoretical properties of mathematical models of computation.

- **[[1.1 Finite Automata|Finite Automata]]:** Applied extensively in text processing, compiler construction, and hardware design.
- **[[2.1 Context-Free Grammars|Context-Free Grammars]]:** Used to define programming language syntax and natural language processing in AI.
- **Practical Significance:** Develops essential practice with formal definitions while providing foundational tools used across non-theoretical computer science.
